How they fit together

Now

Concept 1 costs little and can be undone. Living in the back for a while also tells you how much you really want concept 2.

One project

Concepts 2 and 3 share the same trades (an engineer, framing, drywall, plumbing and electrical), so they're cheaper and less disruptive done together. The bath rebuild is already on the list because the surround is failing. Concept 5 is a bolder version of concept 2's stairwell, so choose between them before the framing starts.

Or go all in

The dream plans (6 to 13) replace concepts 1–5 rather than adding to them, so pick one. If that's where you want to end up, skip the in-between steps it would undo, and phase it: finish the basement first so you have somewhere to live, then do the main floor in one go.

When it's due

Concept 4 waits for the furnace or water heater to need replacing. Ask the HVAC contractor about direct-vent units when you get quotes.

Also worth a look

Light in the hall

The hall has no windows. A solar tube in the hall ceiling (about $600–1,000 installed) brings in daylight, and the ceiling already needs patching.

The basement

It's unfinished and already plumbed for a second bath. Finishing it is the biggest space gain available: a second bathroom, plus a rec room or bedrooms. It needs its own measured plan first.

The end of the hall

The linen closet door swings out into the hall between the two bedroom doors. A bifold door, or open shelving, would make that end feel less crowded.
